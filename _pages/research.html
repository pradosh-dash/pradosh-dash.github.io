Research

I work on acoustic systems that have to perform through difficult media: the human skull, the skin, and the structure of a machine. My research spans four connected areas: transcranial focused ultrasound for non-invasive brain therapy, wearable ultrasound sensing, vibroacoustics and NVH, and ultrasound neuromodulation. I'm drawn to problems at the boundaries between disciplines, where new perspectives can unlock progress, so I combine wave physics, solid mechanics, signal processing, and hardware, using careful experiments and modeling to understand how a system really works. Rather than optimizing isolated components, I think in terms of complete solutions, taking ideas from first concept to a validated, practical outcome through clear mechanisms, thoughtful design, and iterative refinement.

Research Areas

Ph.D. research · Georgia Tech

Transcranial Focused Ultrasound

Focusing ultrasound through the skull for non-invasive brain therapy. I design acoustic holograms and lens topologies that correct skull aberration, built on a heterogeneous angular spectrum approach, and use the parametric array effect to listen to the therapy from outside the head.

Industry research · Meta Reality Labs

Wearable Ultrasound Sensing

Compact ultrasound sensing for AR/VR wearables: transducer arrays, beamforming, and real-time DSP that fit strict size, power, and cost limits. I developed Doppler-based motion rejection and SVD clutter filtering so the sensor keeps working while the wearer moves.
