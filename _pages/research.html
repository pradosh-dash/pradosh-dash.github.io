Research

I work on acoustic systems that have to perform through difficult media: the human skull, the skin, and the structure of a machine. My work connects wave physics, signal processing, and hardware, from transcranial focused ultrasound therapy to wearable sensing and product NVH.

I'm particularly drawn to problems that sit at the boundaries between disciplines, where new perspectives can unlock progress. My research is rooted in connecting fundamental scientific understanding with meaningful real-world impact. I integrate insights from wave physics, solid mechanics, and vibroacoustics, using careful experimentation and modeling to understand complex systems at a deep level. Rather than focusing on isolated components, I think in terms of complete solutions, following ideas from initial concept to validated, practical outcome. My work emphasizes clarity of mechanism, thoughtful design, and iterative refinement, which lets me navigate challenges that span multiple domains.

Research Areas

Ph.D. research · Georgia Tech

Transcranial Focused Ultrasound

Focusing ultrasound through the skull for non-invasive brain therapy. I design acoustic holograms and lens topologies that correct skull aberration, built on a heterogeneous angular spectrum approach, and use the parametric array effect to listen to the therapy from outside the head.

Industry research · Meta Reality Labs

Wearable Ultrasound Sensing

Compact ultrasound sensing for AR/VR wearables: transducer arrays, beamforming, and real-time DSP that fit strict size, power, and cost limits. I developed Doppler-based motion rejection and SVD clutter filtering so the sensor keeps working while the wearer moves.
